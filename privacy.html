Privacy Policy

For Brick Brawl, Daisy Grid, Pour Palette and Word Grove by Noctizzle · Effective 6 October 2026

The short version

  • The games never ask for your name, email address, phone number or a password. None of them ask for access to your contacts, photos, files, camera, microphone or location.
  • Your progress and coins are saved on your phone.
  • All four games show ads from Google AdMob. Google collects device and usage information to show and measure ads and, where the law requires it, the game asks for your consent first. See Google's business data responsibility site.

It then uses an anonymous online profile (not linked to your name, email or phone number) with a randomly generated player name. Other players see that name, your trophies and your scores. You can delete the profile in the game at any time, and it is deleted automatically within 30 days of the last time the game connected to it.

3. Information saved on your phone

Each game saves its progress on your phone. This includes your coins, settings such as vibration, your daily-reward streak and the game's own progress, such as boards, levels, scores and unlocked cosmetics. It also includes a few counters used to space out ads, such as when the last full-screen ad was shown. Brick Brawl also saves your player name, your recent arcade scores and play statistics, and, if you play online, the sign-in token for your online profile.

Apart from what Brick Brawl sends when you play online (section 5), this information isn't sent to us. Depending on your settings, Android may include it in your phone's backup to your Google account. It stays on your phone until you clear the game's storage (in your phone's Settings app → Apps → the game → Storage) or uninstall the game.

4. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The games are free because they show ads: banner ads, full-screen ads at natural breaks (such as after a game ends) and optional reward videos that you choose to watch for a bonus. The ads are served by Google AdMob, a service of Google.

What Google collects

When the game requests ads, Google's Mobile Ads software in the game collects:

  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate your approximate location;
  • your device's advertising ID and app set ID, and basic device information such as the model, operating system and language;
  • interactions with the game and with ads, such as app launches, ad views and taps;
  • diagnostic information, such as how quickly the app starts and how it performs.

Google uses this information to show and measure ads, for analytics and to prevent fraud. Google decides how it uses this information, under its own privacy policy (Google is an independent controller). Google may also pass information from ad requests, such as your IP address and advertising ID, to other advertising companies that bid to show ads through Google. In the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, these ad partners are listed in the game's consent message, and they only receive information as your choices there allow. We only receive aggregated reports, such as how many ads were shown; we don't receive information that identifies you.

Your consent (UK, European Economic Area and Switzerland)

If you are in the UK, the European Economic Area or Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent message the first time it opens, before any ads load. You can accept or decline personalised ads, or choose by purpose. If you decline, you will still see ads, but they won't be based on your past activity; they may still use your approximate location and the game you're playing.

Each time a game starts, it checks with Google whether a consent message needs to be shown, and that check sends basic device information to Google.

Changing your choice

  • Where the law requires it, a Privacy Choices button appears in the game's Settings (tap the gear icon). Use it to change or withdraw your consent at any time. Withdrawing consent stops future use, but doesn't change anything that happened before you withdrew it.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's Settings app (usually Privacy → Ads or Google → Ads, depending on your phone).
  • You can manage the ads Google shows you at My Ad Center.

US state privacy laws

In US states with privacy laws, ads may be personalised unless you opt out. Under some of these laws, personalised advertising may count as "selling" or "sharing" personal information, or as "targeted advertising". To opt out, tap Privacy Choices in the game's Settings and choose to opt out. Google then shows you only non-personalised ads in that game.

Ad content

We ask Google to show only ads rated PG (suitable for most audiences, with parental guidance). The games are made for players aged 13 and over, so we don't mark them as directed at children.

5. Brick Brawl online profile

Brick Brawl's online duels and leaderboards use Supabase, a database service that stores the information below on our behalf.

When the profile is created

Brick Brawl only goes online when you choose to. The first time you tap Ranked duel or the global leaderboards, the game asks whether you want to play online. If you agree, it signs in to an anonymous online profile. You don't enter an email address or a password. The game picks a random player name for you, such as "NeonFox42". You can't type your own name, so it never contains your real name. Arcade mode and practice duels against the bot work without going online.

What is stored on the server

  • a random ID for your anonymous sign-in;
  • your player name and chosen ball trail (a cosmetic);
  • your trophies (your duel rating), wins, losses, draws and win streak;
  • your arcade scores and the wave you reached, with the time each was submitted (while you're online, a score is sent automatically when an arcade run ends with a score above zero);
  • your ranked duels: who you played, the scores, the trophy changes and when they happened;
  • while you search for an opponent, a matchmaking entry with your trophies and the version of the duel rules your game uses (so you're only matched with players on the same rules);
  • when your profile was created and when the game last connected to it.

Your profile, scores and duel records don't include your IP address, advertising ID, device ID, location or contact details. Separately, Supabase's sign-in service keeps a record of your current sign-in session, including the IP address and device information used to sign in. It also keeps short-lived technical logs for security and troubleshooting (see section 10).

Who can see it

  • Leaderboards show your rank, player name, ball trail and score or trophies to anyone using Brick Brawl, so treat them as public.
  • In a ranked duel, your opponent sees your player name, trophies and ball trail. During the duel the two phones exchange live game information through Supabase: your player name, trophies and ball trail, the version of the duel rules your game uses (so two different game versions don't play an unfair match), and your score, combo, wave, board layout, attacks and final score. These live messages aren't stored.

The server checks that submitted scores are plausible and limits how often they can be sent, to keep the leaderboards fair.

Deleting your profile

In Brick Brawl, go to the Home screen and tap the gear icon (top left) to open Settings. Tap Delete online profile, then Delete it to confirm. (This button appears only while online play is on.) This removes your anonymous sign-in, your profile, your arcade scores and every ranked duel you played, including from your opponents' history. Your opponents' trophies aren't recalculated. After you delete it, Brick Brawl stays offline: it won't create a new profile or send scores unless you choose to play online again.

Your profile is also deleted automatically, with everything linked to it, within 30 days of the last time Brick Brawl connected to it. While online play is on, opening the game connects to it. If you uninstall the game or clear its storage, it can't connect any more, so you don't need to reinstall it or contact us. If Brick Brawl later finds that its profile was deleted, it goes offline and asks you again before making a new one. Coins, unlocks and statistics saved on your phone stay there until you clear the game's storage or uninstall it. See Delete your Brick Brawl online profile.

Where it is stored

Brick Brawl's database is stored on Amazon Web Services servers in London, in the United Kingdom. Supabase's short-lived technical logs, which can include IP addresses, may be processed in the United States (see section 9).

6. Remove Ads purchases

A game may offer Remove Ads, a one-time purchase that turns off banner and full-screen ads. Google Play handles the payment, so we never see your card or payment details.

When a game offers Remove Ads, it connects to RevenueCat, a purchase-management service, each time it starts. It does this to check whether you own Remove Ads and to show its price, even if you never buy it. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app user ID that it creates and basic device and connection information (such as your IP address, operating system, app version and language). If you buy Remove Ads, it also receives your purchase history in that game: the product, the date and the Google Play purchase receipt. Your purchase status is also saved on your phone. Use Restore Purchases in the game's Settings to restore it on a new phone.

Optional reward videos stay available after you buy Remove Ads, so Google's ads software described in section 4 keeps running and may still process the information described there.

9. International transfers

Brick Brawl's online database is hosted in London. The UK is covered by an EU adequacy decision, so information from players in the European Economic Area can be stored there without extra safeguards. Google, Supabase and RevenueCat are based in the United States and may process information there or elsewhere.

When personal information leaves the UK or the European Economic Area, these providers protect it with legal safeguards set out in their data processing terms. These include the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension where they are certified, and the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses with the UK International Data Transfer Addendum. You can ask us for a copy of these safeguards at noctizzle@gmail.com.

11. Security

Information sent between the games and Google, Supabase and RevenueCat is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Players can't read or change Brick Brawl's database tables directly; every change goes through server functions that check who is asking, and trophies are worked out on the server. Live duel messages travel on a channel named after a random match ID that only the two players are given, and they aren't stored. We don't collect passwords or payment details, and only we can access the services' admin consoles.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your information, we will report it to the authorities when the law requires it and tell affected players. Because we don't have your contact details, we will usually do this with a notice on this page or in a game update.

12. Your rights

If you are in the UK or the European Economic Area, you have the right to access your personal information and to have it corrected or deleted. You can also restrict how it is used, get a copy in a portable format, and withdraw consent at any time (for ads, use Privacy Choices in the game's Settings).

Right to object. You can object at any time to how we use your information based on our legitimate interests, including Brick Brawl's online profile. To object, delete your online profile in the game (Settings, then Delete online profile). Brick Brawl then stays offline until you choose to play online again. You can also email us.

If you live in a US state with a privacy law, you have the right to know what personal information we hold, to get a copy, and to have it corrected or deleted. You can also opt out of its sale or sharing for targeted advertising using Privacy Choices in the game's Settings. We won't treat you differently for using these rights. We don't collect sensitive personal information.

In the last 12 months the games have collected: identifiers (advertising ID, app set ID, IP address and, for Brick Brawl online players, a sign-in ID and player name); internet or other network activity (interactions with the games and ads); approximate location worked out from your IP address; and Brick Brawl game records (scores, trophies and duels). They come from you and your device. Purposes, recipients and retention are in sections 4 to 10. We don't knowingly sell or share the personal information of consumers under 16.

How to use your rights

Email noctizzle@gmail.com. The quickest way to delete Brick Brawl's online profile is in the game, and it is deleted automatically within 30 days of the last time the game connected to it. Because the games don't use accounts with an email address, we may ask for details that help us find your information. For Daisy Grid, Pour Palette and Word Grove we hold no information about you ourselves, apart from a Remove Ads purchase record at RevenueCat if you bought it (section 6); everything else is saved on your phone or held by Google for ads (see section 4 for Google's tools).

We reply within one month. If a request is complex we may need up to two more months, and we will tell you if so. You can use an authorised agent; we may ask them for proof of your permission. If we refuse your request, you can appeal by replying to our answer with the subject "Appeal", and we will respond within 45 days.

Complaints

If you're unhappy with how we handle your information, you can complain to us by emailing noctizzle@gmail.com with the subject "Data protection complaint". Tell us what happened and which game it is about. We will acknowledge your complaint within 30 days, look into it, keep you updated and tell you the outcome without undue delay.

You can also complain to a data protection authority at any time: in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in the European Economic Area, the authority in your country (list of authorities).

13. Children and teenagers

The games are made for players aged 13 and over and are not directed at children under 13. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has a Brick Brawl online profile, email us and we will delete it.

If you're under 18: Brick Brawl only goes online if you choose to, your profile uses a random name rather than your real one, and you can delete it at any time in Settings. You can say no to personalised ads in the consent message or later in Privacy Choices, and you can still play everything.
